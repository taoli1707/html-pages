How does a ceramic coating for a car work?

Written 2026-10-11 · Reading time: about 30 minutes · You need to know: what a car paint layer is, and what a chemical bond is

The short answer

A ceramic coating is a liquid that you wipe on the paint. It contains a silicon resin (in the products on this page, a polysilazane) in 85% to 95% solvent. The solvent evaporates. Then water from the air converts the resin into a hard network of silicon and oxygen atoms, which is chemically similar to glass. This network bonds to the clear coat, the transparent top layer of the paint.

The result is a film of about 0.2 µm per coat. That is 200 times thinner than the 40 µm clear coat, and 26 to 45 times thicker than a wax film. Organic groups on the outside of the film give it a low surface energy. Therefore water forms round drops with a contact angle of about 105° to 110°. Waxes give 71° to 95°, and bare clear coat about 78°. Water and dirt then adhere less well.

The "9H" on the bottle is a pencil hardness grade, not a value on the Mohs scale of minerals. A 9H coating resists a 9H pencil. It does not resist sand, keys, or stone chips.

Assumptions
  • "Ceramic coating" means a consumer product for car paint with silicon dioxide (SiO2) chemistry. The page uses three products as examples: Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO, Gtechniq Crystal Serum Light, and CarPro CQuartz UK 3.0. Other products in this class are similar but not identical.
  • The painted area of one car is 10 m². This is an example value. The page states where it matters.
  • The durability claims of the manufacturers are stated, not verified. No independent test with published numbers was found.

1. One coat on one car: 20 ml of liquid becomes a film of 0.2 µm

Car paint has four layers. The top layer is the clear coat: a transparent, hard layer of about 40 µm that protects the colour layer below it. A ceramic coating is a liquid product that you wipe on the clear coat. It leaves a very thin, hard, water-repellent film that is chemically bonded to the clear coat.

The word "ceramic" is a marketing word: the film is a glass-like network of silicon and oxygen atoms with organic groups attached.

The first question is: how much material stays on the car? The example uses the published numbers of one product, Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO. The retailer states a consumption of 15 to 25 ml per car. It also states a film thickness (the thickness of the solid layer after the solvent has left) of 0.2 µm per coat.

Worked example: one coat of Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO on a car with 10 m² of paint
  1. You spread 20 ml of liquid over 10 m². 20 ml is 20 cm³, or 0.000020 m³. The wet film is 0.000020 m³ ÷ 10 m² = 0.0000020 m = 2.0 µm. Volume divided by area gives the thickness. 1 ml = 0.000001 m³, and 1 µm = 0.000001 m.
  2. The solvent evaporates. The retailer states that the dry film is 0.2 µm. So the solid film is 0.2 µm ÷ 2.0 µm = 10% of the wet film. The solids stay, the solvent leaves. The area is the same, so the ratio of thicknesses is the ratio of volumes.
  3. The solid volume on the car is 0.2 µm × 10 m² = 0.0000002 m × 10 m² = 0.000002 m³ = 2 ml. The other 18 ml evaporated. Thickness multiplied by area gives the volume.
  4. Check with a second product. The safety data sheet of Gtechniq Crystal Serum Light lists 85% to 95% petroleum distillates, which is the solvent. So 5% to 15% of that product is solid resin. The 10% of step 2 lies inside this range. Two independent numbers agree. With 15 ml the solids would be 13.3%, with 25 ml 8.0%.
  5. Compare with the clear coat: 40 µm ÷ 0.2 µm = 200. One coat is 200 times thinner than the clear coat. The clear coat thickness is 40 µm in a typical four-layer paint system.
  6. Compare with wax. A study measured the film of four car waxes and sealants: 4.5 nm to 7.8 nm, that is 0.0045 µm to 0.0078 µm. 0.2 µm ÷ 0.0078 µm = 25.8, and 0.2 µm ÷ 0.0045 µm = 44.5. The ceramic coating is 26 to 45 times thicker than a wax film. The wax films were measured with ellipsometry on polished silicon wafers.

If the painted area is 8 m² instead of 10 m², the wet film is 2.5 µm and the solids are 8%. The dry film of 0.2 µm does not change.

2. The chemistry: water from the air turns polysilazane into a glass-like network

The solid part of the liquid is a polysilazane. A polysilazane is a chain molecule in which silicon (Si) and nitrogen (N) atoms alternate: –Si–N–Si–N–. The simplest form is perhydropolysilazane (from here: PHPS), with the repeat unit [–SiH2–NH–]. Every silicon carries two hydrogen atoms, and every nitrogen carries one. The Gyeon retailer page names PHPS as the main component of Q2 Mohs EVO, with organic copolymers and poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA).

PHPS is useful because it reacts with water at room temperature. A study on the protection of old glass describes the reaction. PHPS reacts with atmospheric moisture (Si-H and Si-NH bonds are hydrolysed to Si-O) and a silica film is produced. In plain words: water from the air replaces the nitrogen and the hydrogen on the silicon with oxygen. The result is silica, the chemical name for SiO2, the material of glass and quartz.

The reaction has two steps. The first step is hydrolysis: a water molecule (H2O) breaks a bond and attaches an OH group to the silicon. The Si–N bond and the Si–H bond both react this way, and each gives a silanol group, Si–OH. The second step is condensation: two Si–OH groups join, they release one water molecule, and they form a siloxane bond, Si–O–Si. The siloxane bond is the bond that holds glass together. Many siloxane bonds in all directions form a three-dimensional network.

The net reaction for PHPS is: [SiH2–NH] + 2 H2O → SiO2 + NH3 + 2 H2. Ammonia (NH3) and hydrogen (H2) leave as gases.

The condensation step also explains the bond to the paint. The surface of a clear coat carries OH groups and other polar groups. A Si–OH group of the coating can condense with such a group. The result is a chemical bond between the film and the paint. A wax, in contrast, only lies on the paint and holds through weak forces. Therefore a wax washes off in weeks, and a coating stays for months or years.

The whole process from liquid to hard film is called curing. Curing needs water, so it needs humidity in the air. It also needs time. The glass study reports that the conversion of PHPS to silica at room temperature took 66 hours. A weak heat treatment at 45 °C to 50 °C with ammonia vapour reduced the time to 2 hours, a factor of 33.

Car products are formulated for faster curing. The retailer of Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO states: 1 hour between coats, 24 hours to harden, and about 14 days to full cure. The sheet for CarPro CQuartz UK 3.0 states: wipe off the excess after 1 to 2 minutes, and wait 60 minutes before the next coat. The diagram shows the sequence.

The safety data sheet of CarPro CQuartz UK 3.0 lists 1% to 3% titanium tetraisopropanolate. Titanium compounds of this type are catalysts for condensation: they make the Si–O–Si bonds form faster. It also lists 1% to 3% hexamethyldisiloxane, a small silicon molecule with methyl groups (CH3). Such molecules leave methyl groups on the surface. The next section shows why this matters.

3. Water beading: a low surface energy raises the contact angle from 78° to about 108°

Here is a surprise. Pure silica is not water-repellent: water on clean glass spreads out completely. The water beading of a ceramic coating comes from organic groups, in particular methyl groups, on the outside of the Si–O–Si network.

The measure of water beading is the contact angle. The contact angle is the angle between the surface and the edge of a water drop, measured inside the drop, in degrees. A flat drop has a small angle, a round drop has a large angle. A surface with a contact angle above 90° is called hydrophobic (water-repellent). Below 90° it is hydrophilic. Above 150° it is superhydrophobic.

The contact angle follows from the surface energy of the solid. Surface energy is the energy that is needed to create one square metre of new surface, in millijoules per square metre (mJ/m²). For a liquid, the same quantity is called surface tension, in millinewtons per metre (mN/m). The two units are equal. Water has a surface tension of 72.8 mN/m at 20 °C.

Glass has a surface energy of 83.4 mJ/m², higher than water, so water spreads on glass: contact angle 0°. Silicone (PDMS) has 20.1 mJ/m², and PTFE has 19.4 mJ/m². On these low-energy surfaces water stays in a drop: 107.2° on PDMS and 109.2° on PTFE. Methyl groups give PDMS its low surface energy. The same methyl groups on a coating give the coating its contact angle.

Guess first

A study measured four car waxes and sealants on a polished silicon wafer. The lowest and the highest water contact angle differed by how many degrees? Write your guess down. The answer is in the next paragraph.

The study measured initial contact angles from 71.17° to 94.85°, a range of about 24°. Only two of the four products passed 90°, the limit for hydrophobic. After one week in storage, the angles had changed by less than 3°. The authors did not measure a longer interval.

The example log of an instrument maker shows what a ceramic coating does on a black clear coat panel. The angle is 78.0° with no coating and 108.5° with a fresh, cured coating. The maker states that these are example values, not customer data. The manufacturer of Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO claims more than 110°.

Pure silica film

Surface energy 83.4 mJ/m², above the 72.8 mN/m of water.

Water spreads: contact angle 0°. No beading.

Silica film with methyl groups on top

Surface energy about 20 mJ/m², far below water.

Water beads: contact angle about 107° to 110°.

The principle: the hard Si–O–Si network gives the durability, and the organic groups on the outside give the water beading. The two properties come from two different parts of the same film. So a coating can lose its beading before it loses its hardness, because dirt covers the organic groups.

The example log shows this. After road exposure with no wash, the angle fell to 86.0°. After a decontamination wash it rose again to 102.5°. After a machine polish it fell to 79.5°, close to the 78.0° of bare clear coat, because the polish removed the film.

4. Wrong idea corrected: "9H" is a pencil grade, and the coating is not as hard as a mineral

Two hardness scales appear here. Pencil hardness is the grade of the hardest pencil that does not mark a paint film in a standard test. The Mohs scale is a scale for minerals from 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond): a mineral scratches every mineral with a lower number.

A common wrong idea

Wrong: "9H" means a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale, almost as hard as sapphire (9) or diamond (10). So the coating stops scratches.

Correct: "9H" is a grade on the pencil hardness scale for paint films. A 9H coating is the coating that a 9H pencil lead does not mark. Pencil lead is graphite with clay. The coating is far softer than quartz sand, which has a Mohs hardness of 7 and is a main cause of wash scratches.

Why the wrong idea fails: the two scales measure different things with different methods. The confusion is so common that one manufacturer writes "Mohs hardness rating of 10h" in its marketing (source 19). A Mohs value has no letter.

The pencil hardness test is defined in the standards ISO 15184 and ASTM D3363. The tester pushes a pencil over the film at an angle of 45° with a fixed force. The standard kit uses 14 pencil grades from 6B (softest) to 6H (hardest). The test cart of one instrument maker pushes with 7.35 N.

Pencil grades up to 9H and 10H exist, but the standard kit stops at 6H. So "9H" and "10H" are claims that the manufacturers made with pencils outside the standard range. The retailer of Gyeon Q2 Mohs EVO states that each coat adds about two grades (2H) to the pencil hardness of the paint. The Mohs scale, in contrast, is a scratch test between solid materials, with no pencil and no fixed force. The table shows objects that a car meets, on the Mohs scale.

What the hardness does give you: a harder film resists the fine scratches from a wash cloth better than the softer clear coat below it. It does not resist a key, a stone, or sand under a dry cloth. The coating also wears with use.

5. Limits: durability claims are not verified, and the film does not stop stone chips

Limits of this explanation
  • Durability has no independent number. The claims of 36 months (Gyeon, one coat) and nine years (Gtechniq Crystal Serum Ultra) come from the manufacturers. The only independent measurement on this page is the wax study, which covered one week. The contact angles of the clear coat and the coating are example values of an instrument maker.
  • The exact resin differs between products. The Gyeon product names PHPS. Other products use polysilazanes with organic side groups, or siloxane resins. All of them form Si–O–Si networks, but the ratio of organic to inorganic parts differs, and so do hardness and beading.
  • The film thickness is a manufacturer value. No third-party measurement of the 0.2 µm was found.
  • The coating does not protect against stone chips, dents, or deep scratches. A 0.2 µm film cannot absorb the energy of a stone. For that, people use a paint protection film of several hundred micrometres.
  • Water spots still form. When hard water dries on the film, the minerals stay. Mineral deposits can etch the film if they stay long.
  • Application errors are permanent. After the cure, only a machine polish removes the film. A spot with too much product, a "high spot", leaves a visible mark.

6. Words that you will hear

These terms appear in product descriptions and in detailing shops. They are not needed for the main idea, but you will meet them.

Sealant
A synthetic polymer film, applied like a wax, that lies on the paint without a chemical bond. In the wax study, the sealant had a film of 5.6 nm and a contact angle of 73°. It lasts longer than a natural wax, but it is not a ceramic coating.
SiO2 spray, topper, or booster
A diluted spray product with silicon resin, applied after a wash. It adds a very thin layer with fresh organic groups on top of the coating, so the contact angle rises again. It does not replace the coating.
Sacrificial layer
The idea that the coating takes the damage instead of the clear coat. This is correct for chemical attack and fine scratches, because the film is on top and can be replaced. It is not correct for stone chips.
Paint correction
Machine polishing of the clear coat before the coating, to remove scratches. The coating bonds to the surface as it is, so every scratch below the coating stays visible for years.
Decontamination
Removal of bonded dirt before the coating: iron particles with a chemical, tar with a solvent, and the rest with a clay bar. Dirt between the film and the clear coat prevents the bond.
Panel wipe or IPA wipe
A wipe with isopropyl alcohol or a special solvent before the coating. It removes polish oils, so the Si–OH groups of the coating can reach the clear coat.
Flash time
The time between the application and the wipe-off, 1 to 2 minutes for CarPro CQuartz UK 3.0. In this time the solvent evaporates and the film begins to cure.
High spot
A place where too much product stayed and cured. It looks like a smear or a rainbow. It must be polished off and coated again.
Water spots
Mineral deposits from hard water that dried on the surface. The USGS classifies water above 180 mg/L of calcium carbonate as very hard. Such water leaves visible deposits on every surface, coated or not.
Sliding angle
The angle to which you tilt the surface until a drop starts to move. A low sliding angle means that drops roll off easily and take dirt with them. In the example log, the fresh coating had a sliding angle of 14°.
Paint protection film (PPF)
A transparent polyurethane film of about 150 to 200 µm that is glued on the paint. It is the product that stops stone chips. Many shops apply a ceramic coating on top of the film. The thickness value is not verified on this page.
Graphene coating
A marketing name for a ceramic coating with a small amount of graphene oxide added. The chemistry of the film is still a Si–O–Si network. No independent measurement of a benefit was found for this page.

7. Check your understanding

Try to answer before you open each answer.
  1. You apply 20 ml of a coating to a car with 12 m² of paint. The dry film is 0.2 µm. How much solid material stays on the car, and what fraction of the liquid is that?
    Show the answer

    2.4 ml, which is 12% of the 20 ml.

    Volume = thickness × area = 0.0000002 m × 12 m² = 0.0000024 m³ = 2.4 ml. Fraction = 2.4 ÷ 20 = 0.12. A typical wrong answer is 2 ml, the value for 10 m² in section 1.

  2. A coated panel shows a water contact angle of 86° after a month on the road. Has the coating failed? What do you do first?
    Show the answer

    You do not know yet. Wash and decontaminate the panel first, then measure again.

    Dirt covers the organic groups that give the low surface energy, so the angle falls although the Si–O–Si network is still there. In the example log, the angle rose from 86.0° to 102.5° after a decontamination wash. The typical wrong answer is "the coating is gone". That is only true if the angle stays low after the wash. See section 3.

  3. Why does a film of pure silica not bead water, although a ceramic coating is mostly silica?
    Show the answer

    Because pure silica has a high surface energy, and the beading comes from organic groups on the surface.

    Glass has a surface energy of 83.4 mJ/m², above the 72.8 mN/m of water, so water spreads on it. Methyl groups, as in silicone, give about 20 mJ/m² and a contact angle of about 107°. A coating puts such groups on the outside of its network. See section 3.

  4. A friend says: "My coating is 9H, so sand cannot scratch it." Which two facts show that this is wrong?
    Show the answer

    First: 9H is a pencil grade, not a Mohs value. Second: quartz sand has a Mohs hardness of 7, which is harder than glass (5.5) and than any paint film.

    The pencil test compares the film with graphite pencil leads. Sand is a mineral and is far harder than any pencil lead. A harder film helps against fine wash marks, not against sand or keys. See section 4.

8. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  • A ceramic coating is a silicon resin, in the products on this page a polysilazane, in 85% to 95% solvent. Water from the air converts it by hydrolysis and condensation into a Si–O–Si network that bonds to the clear coat.
  • One coat is about 0.2 µm. That is 200 times thinner than the 40 µm clear coat. It is 26 to 45 times thicker than a wax film of 4.5 to 7.8 nm.
  • Water beading comes from organic groups on the outside of the film, not from the silica. They lower the surface energy to about 20 mJ/m², and the contact angle rises from 78° to about 108°.
  • "9H" is a pencil hardness grade, not a Mohs value. Sand (Mohs 7), keys, and stones scratch the film. The durability claims of 3 to 9 years come from manufacturers and are not verified.

Glossary

Ceramic coating
A liquid product for car paint that leaves a very thin, hard, water-repellent film of silicon and oxygen, chemically bonded to the clear coat.
Clear coat
The transparent, hard top layer of car paint, about 40 µm thick.
Condensation
The reaction in which two Si–OH groups join, release a water molecule, and form a siloxane bond.
Contact angle
The angle between a surface and the edge of a water drop, measured inside the drop. Larger means rounder drops.
Curing
The whole process in which the liquid becomes a hard film: solvent evaporation, hydrolysis, and condensation.
Film thickness
The thickness of the solid layer after the solvent has left, here in micrometres.
Hydrolysis
The reaction in which a water molecule breaks a bond and attaches an OH group, here to a silicon atom.
Hydrophobic
Water-repellent: a surface with a water contact angle above 90°.
Mohs scale
A hardness scale for minerals from 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond), based on which mineral scratches which.
Pencil hardness
The grade of the hardest pencil that does not mark a paint film in the test of ISO 15184 and ASTM D3363.
Polysilazane
A chain molecule of alternating silicon and nitrogen atoms. Perhydropolysilazane (PHPS) is the form with only hydrogen on the chain.
Siloxane bond
The bond Si–O–Si that holds glass, quartz, and a cured ceramic coating together.
Surface energy
The energy that is needed to create one square metre of new surface, in mJ/m². For a liquid it is called surface tension, in mN/m.
