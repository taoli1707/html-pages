Which be quiet! 900 case should you choose: Dark Base Pro 901, Light Base 900 FX, or Light Base 900 DX?

Written 2026-10-11 · Reading time: about 30 minutes · You need to know: the parts of a desktop PC (motherboard, CPU cooler, graphics card, power supply)

The short answer

be quiet! sells three cases with "900" in the name in October 2026. All three are full towers (cases larger than a mid-tower, for the largest motherboards and coolers). They hold graphics cards up to 495 mm and CPU coolers up to 190 mm. They differ in purpose, not in cooling.

Dark Base Pro 901 (street price €339.90) is the quiet workstation case. It has closed panels with insulation mats, three Silent Wings 4 fans, a 5.25" bay and room for 7 hard drives. In the HWBusters test it reduced sound from inside by 9.6 dB(A), the best value of the three.

Light Base 900 FX (€239.90) is the show case. It has glass on the front and the side, a light strip around three sides and four lit fans. You can turn the body upside down or lay it flat. Light Base 900 DX (€179.90) is the same body without the four fans.

Measured in one lab: at equal noise, the three cases and the Shadow Base 800 FX differed by at most 1.25 °C on the CPU. On the GPU the difference was at most 2.03 °C. The cheapest case was the coolest. So buy a 900 case for space, silence or looks, not for lower temperatures.

1. One example: the same build fits all three, with 191 mm and 22 mm to spare

A case is sold by its clearance. The clearance is the largest part that fits in a given place, for example the longest graphics card or the highest CPU cooler. A full tower is a case with more clearance and more fan positions than a mid-tower such as the Shadow Base 800 FX.

All three accept an E-ATX motherboard. E-ATX (Extended ATX) is a motherboard format up to 330 mm wide, instead of the 244 mm of ATX. They also accept a radiator of up to 420 mm. A radiator is the part of a liquid cooler that gives the heat to the air. Its size is the sum of the fans on it: a 420 mm radiator carries three 140 mm fans.

Worked example: does the RTX 4090 build fit?
  1. Graphics card: 495 mm − 304 mm = 191 mm to spare in every 900 case. In the Shadow Base 800 FX the figure is 430 mm − 304 mm = 126 mm. The three datasheets give the same card limit of 495 mm.
  2. CPU cooler: 190 mm − 168 mm = 22 mm to spare in every 900 case, against 12 mm in the Shadow Base 800 FX. All three datasheets give 190 mm. The Light Base 900 is 80 mm wider than the Shadow Base 800 FX, but its rear chamber uses part of the width.
  3. Power supply: the Dark Base Pro 901 takes units up to 288 mm, the Light Base 900 only up to 225 mm. In the Light Base 900 the power supply stands in the rear chamber, behind the motherboard, so the space is shorter.
  4. A 420 mm radiator fits in the front of the Dark Base Pro 901. In the Light Base 900 it fits in the top or in the bottom. The Dark Base Pro 901 takes 360 mm at most in the top. The Light Base 900 takes two 420 mm radiators at the same time.

Result: the build fits everywhere. The 900 cases add 65 mm of card length and 10 mm of cooler height over the Shadow Base 800 FX. For this build, the extra clearance buys nothing.

2. The facts: size, limits, what is in the box, and prices

The three 900 cases have about the same volume, but different shapes. The Dark Base Pro 901 is the classic shape: tall and long, with the power supply under a shroud at the bottom. The Light Base 900 has a dual-chamber layout. A dual-chamber layout puts the motherboard in a front chamber behind glass. The power supply, the cables and the drives sit in a second chamber behind the motherboard. The case becomes shorter and lower, but wider.

3. Measured: temperatures within 2 °C, damping from 5.5 to 9.6 dB(A)

The site HWBusters tests every case on the same system in a climate chamber. The system is an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D with a Noctua NH-D15S air cooler and a GeForce RTX 4060, at 25 °C. The Light Base 900 FX review (December 2024) shows the Dark Base Pro 901 and the Shadow Base 800 FX in the same charts. One limit: the Dark Base Pro 901 was tested in its silent configuration, with the closed panels in place.

HWBusters measures noise as a sound pressure level in dB(A). The sound pressure level is a logarithmic scale of the pressure of a sound wave. The A-weighting lowers the weight of very low and very high frequencies, as the human ear does.

The lab sets the fans of each case so that the whole case produces 25 dB(A) at 1 m. This is a noise-normalised test: a test at equal noise, so that a case with louder fans gets no advantage. Then the lab runs a torture test. A torture test loads the CPU with Prime95 and the GPU with Furmark at the same time. The temperature in the charts is the average over the test.

Guess first

How to read this table: each row is one test and one part, and the columns are the three cases. "Spread" is the warmest value minus the coolest value in the row. The CPU runs near 89 °C in the torture test, because the 7950X3D is set to hold 89 °C and lowers its clock instead. So HWBusters also reports the clock, and the reviewer wrote that the Light Base 900 FX held a low clock. Full fan speed gains little against the 25 dB(A) setting. The gain is 0.67 °C (Shadow Base 800 FX), 1.00 °C (Dark Base Pro 901) and 0.05 °C (Light Base 900 FX).

The second measurement is noise damping. HWBusters places a speaker inside the closed case and plays two test sounds. Pink noise has equal energy in each octave; a chirp is a tone that sweeps through the frequencies. The damping is the sound pressure level with the case open minus the level with the case closed. A larger number means that the case blocks more of the sound from inside, for example from a hard drive or a pump.

The third measurement is the noise of the included fans, all together at a fixed share of their maximum speed, at 1 m. The Dark Base Pro 901 is the loudest at every setting. Its Silent Wings 4 fans run at 1,900 rpm at full speed and move 40% more air each than a Light Wings fan. At 50% speed the three cases differ by 13.8 dB(A), at 100% by 6.5 dB(A).

4. The comparison: a quiet workstation against a show case

Dark Base Pro 901

Closed steel panels with insulation mats, or open mesh when you swap the panels. Front intake and rear exhaust with three Silent Wings 4 fans. Glass on the side only.

A 5.25" bay, 2 drive cages included and 7 possible, 6 SSD brackets. Touch buttons and a Qi charger on the top. Weight 17.8 kg. Street price €339.90.

Light Base 900 FX

Glass on the front and on the side, no insulation. Side intake through three reversed-blade fans, rear exhaust. A light strip with 172 LEDs, and two hubs with 12 connectors.

The body stands normally, inverted, or horizontally. 2 drive bays for 3.5" and 6 for 2.5". Weight 15.8 kg. Street price €239.90; the DX is the same case without fans for €179.90.

The principle: the two designs solve different problems. The Dark Base Pro 901 reduces the sound that leaves the case. The Light Base 900 shows the parts inside the case. Neither design lowers the temperatures.

5. Recommendation, and why

Recommendation

Choose the Dark Base Pro 901 when the PC must be quiet and must hold many drives. It is the only one of the three with insulation, with a 5.25" bay and with a 288 mm power supply limit. Its fans are the loudest at a given speed setting, so set them to a low curve. Full speed gains only 1.00 °C.

Choose the Light Base 900 FX when you want to see the parts. It costs €100.00 less than the Dark Base Pro 901 and includes four lit fans and two hubs. It still holds 495 mm cards and two 420 mm radiators. Choose the DX only when you already own fans, because the four fans of the FX cost only €60.00 more.

Choose neither for lower temperatures. The Shadow Base 800 FX was cooler than all three in the same lab and costs €139.90. Buy a 900 case for its space, its silence or its looks.

6. Wrong idea corrected: the larger case is not the cooler case

A common wrong idea

Wrong: "A full tower has more fans and more air inside, so it keeps the parts cooler than a mid-tower."

Correct: the temperature of a part depends on the air that passes over its cooler, not on the air that stands in the case. At equal noise the Shadow Base 800 FX (70.9 litres) was coolest. The Light Base 900 FX (84.2 litres) came next, and the Dark Base Pro 901 (87.3 litres) was warmest on the GPU.

Why the wrong idea fails: a larger case needs the fans to push the air a longer way. The Light Base 900 takes its air in through the side, so the air reaches the CPU cooler from an angle. The Dark Base Pro 901 in the silent configuration breathes through small openings around the closed panels. The HWBusters reviewer called the noise-normalised results of the Light Base 900 FX mediocre in torture testing. In plain words: the large open case did not cool better than smaller cases.

7. Limits: one lab, one shop, chart readings

Where this page stops
  • One lab. All temperatures and all noise values come from HWBusters. Other reviews (TweakTown, HWCooling, igor'sLAB) used other systems, so their numbers are not on this page.
  • The Dark Base Pro 901 was tested closed. HWBusters used the silent configuration. With the mesh panels, the temperatures and the damping would both be lower. No measurement of the airflow configuration exists.
  • Chart readings. The temperatures and the damping values are printed on the HWBusters charts, so they are exact. The fan noise lines of the Light Base 900 FX and of the Shadow Base 800 FX are read from line charts. The error is about 0.5 dB(A).
  • Prices. Street prices are one shop on one day. Prices in the United States were not checked in October 2026.
  • Datasheet conflicts. HWBusters lists the Dark Base Pro 901 at 16.3 kg, 603 mm long, with a 325 mm power supply limit. The datasheet (version 2 of 2024) says 17.8 kg, 604 mm and 288 mm. This page follows the datasheet.
  • Hubs of the Dark Base Pro 901. The datasheet lists a fan controller and an ARGB controller. HWBusters found two fan hubs with six headers and no ARGB hub for fans. Check this before you plan lit fans in that case.
  • No test of the Light Base 900 DX. Its results depend on the fans that you add.

8. Words that you will hear

Inverted layout
The motherboard is mounted upside down, so the glass side is on the right. Both the Dark Base Pro 901 and the Light Base 900 support it. It matters when the PC stands to the left of you.
Horizontal layout
The Light Base 900 can lie on its side on four stands. The top and the bottom panels are identical, so the fans and the radiators stay in place.
Panorama design, aquarium case
Shop names for a case with glass on the front and on the side, such as the Light Base 900.
Backside connector motherboards
Motherboards with all cable connectors on the rear side. The Light Base 900 datasheet lists support for them; the Dark Base Pro 901 datasheet does not.
Riser cable, vertical GPU mount
A flexible PCIe cable that lets the graphics card stand upright next to the glass. The Dark Base Pro 901 has four vertical slots; the be quiet! cable costs US$54.90 extra.
HDD Cage 2
The be quiet! drive cage for all three cases, launch price US$9.90. The Light Base 900 takes two of them, one is in the box.
Silent Wings 4, Light Wings
The two be quiet! fan families on this page. Silent Wings 4 fans have no lighting; the 140 mm high-speed model moves 133.2 m³/h at 1,900 rpm. Light Wings fans have ARGB lighting. The 140 mm model moves 95.1 m³/h at 1,500 rpm, the reversed model 87.3 m³/h at 1,300 rpm.

9. Check your understanding

1. You want a PC that is as quiet as possible with two hard drives and a water pump. Which case, and which measured number supports it?

The Dark Base Pro 901. Its noise damping is 9.6 dB(A) for pink noise, against 7.5 for the Light Base 900 FX and 5.5 for the Shadow Base 800 FX. Hard drives and pumps make sound inside the case, and damping measures how much of that sound stays inside. A typical wrong answer picks the Light Base 900 FX because its fans are quieter; but fan noise depends on the fan curve.

2. At 25 dB(A) the CPU ran at 88.05 °C in the Shadow Base 800 FX and 89.30 °C in the Light Base 900 FX. Does the larger case cool better?

No. The difference is 1.25 °C in favour of the smaller case. The CPU in the test is set to hold 89 °C, so a case that cools worse shows the same temperature and a lower clock. The reviewer reported a low clock for the Light Base 900 FX. A typical wrong answer reads the larger volume as better cooling.

3. The Light Base 900 FX costs €60.00 more than the DX. Is the FX a good deal?

Yes, when you want lit be quiet! fans. The four included fans would cost about €110.00 at the recommended price of €27.50 each. No, when you own fans or want another model. Then the DX saves €60.00 and loses nothing else, because the hubs and the light strip are in both. A typical wrong answer forgets that the DX has no fans at all.

4. Your power supply is 240 mm long. Which cases take it?

The Dark Base Pro 901 (limit 288 mm) and the Shadow Base 800 FX (limit 250 mm). The Light Base 900 does not (limit 225 mm), because its power supply stands in the rear chamber. A typical wrong answer assumes that a full tower takes every power supply.

10. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  • Three current 900 cases: Dark Base Pro 901 (€339.90), Light Base 900 FX (€239.90), Light Base 900 DX (€179.90). All take 495 mm cards, 190 mm coolers and 420 mm radiators.
  • In the same lab at equal noise, the four cases differed by at most 1.25 °C on the CPU and 2.03 °C on the GPU. The cheapest case was the coolest.
  • The Dark Base Pro 901 blocks 9.6 dB(A) of sound from inside; the Light Base 900 FX 7.5; the Shadow Base 800 FX 5.5.
  • The Light Base 900 trades insulation and drive bays for glass on two sides, a light strip, and inverted or horizontal layouts. The FX adds four fans for €60.00, which would cost about €110.00 alone.
  • Choose by purpose. Silence and drives: the Dark Base Pro 901. Looks and layout: the Light Base 900. Cooling per euro: the Shadow Base 800 FX.
Clearance
The largest part that fits in a given place in the case.
Dual-chamber layout
A case design with the motherboard in a front chamber and the power supply, the cables and the drives in a chamber behind it.
E-ATX
Extended ATX, a motherboard format up to 330 mm wide.
Full tower
A case with more clearance and more fan positions than a mid-tower.
Insulation mats
Foam sheets glued to the inside of a panel, which absorb sound.
Noise damping
The sound pressure level of a source with the case open minus the level with the source inside the closed case, in dB(A).
Noise-normalised test
A test in which the fans of each case are set so that the case produces the same noise, here 25 dB(A).
Radiator
The part of a liquid cooler that gives the heat to the air; its size is the sum of the fans on it.
Reversed-blade fan
A fan with the blades turned the other way, so that the clean side faces the glass and the air still flows into the case.
Silent configuration
The Dark Base Pro 901 with its closed front panel and closed top cover, both lined with insulation mats.
Sound pressure level
A logarithmic measure of the pressure of a sound wave, in dB; dB(A) weights the frequencies as the human ear does.
Torture test
A test that loads the CPU and the GPU at the same time with Prime95 and Furmark.
