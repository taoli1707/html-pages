What do you get with the be quiet! Shadow Base 800 FX case, and how well does it cool and stay quiet?

Written 2026-10-11 · Reading time: about 30 minutes · You need to know: the parts of a desktop PC (motherboard, CPU cooler, graphics card, power supply)

The short answer

The Shadow Base 800 FX is a large mid-tower case (a case for a standard ATX motherboard) from the German company be quiet!. It is built for airflow. The front and the top are open mesh. Four 140 mm fans with ARGB lighting (lighting where each LED can show its own colour) are included. It holds graphics cards up to 430 mm, CPU coolers up to 180 mm and radiators up to 420 mm.

Measured: in the Tom's Hardware test it ran 7 °C to 10 °C cooler on average than five other cases. At full fan speed it was the quietest of six cases: 39 dB at 60 cm. In the HWBusters test, the step from the quietest fan setting to full speed changed the CPU temperature by only 0.67 °C. So you can run the fans slow.

Price: the launch price was US$219.90 in black and $229.90 in white. The plain Shadow Base 800 cost $169.90 and has the same steel body with three fans, no lighting and no fan hub. Buy the FX for the fourth fan, the lit fans and the 8-port hub. Choose a smaller case when your build has no large graphics card and no 360 mm or 420 mm radiator.

1. One example: an RTX 4090 and a 168 mm cooler fit, with 126 mm and 12 mm to spare

A PC case is sold by its clearance. The clearance is the largest part that fits in a given place: the longest graphics card, the highest CPU cooler, the largest liquid cooler. The Shadow Base 800 FX is a mid-tower. A mid-tower stands upright, holds a standard ATX motherboard and is smaller than a full tower.

It also accepts an E-ATX motherboard. E-ATX (Extended ATX) is a motherboard format up to 330 mm wide, instead of the 244 mm of ATX. And it accepts a radiator of up to 420 mm. A radiator is the part of a liquid cooler that gives the heat to the air. Its size is the sum of the fans on it, so a 420 mm radiator carries three 140 mm fans.

The example build has two large parts. The graphics card is an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 Founders Edition: 304 mm long, 137 mm wide, 3 slots (61 mm) thick. NVIDIA asks for 36 mm more space for the power cable. The CPU cooler is a be quiet! Dark Rock Pro 5: 168 mm high with its fans.

Worked example: does the build fit?
  1. Graphics card length: 430 mm − 304 mm = 126 mm to spare. The limit of 430 mm is for the delivered case, which has the three front fans in place.
  2. Power cable: 126 mm − 36 mm = 90 mm still to spare. NVIDIA asks for 36 mm beyond the card for the cable.
  3. Slots: the card covers 3 of the 7 expansion slots. 4 slots remain. The case has 7 slot covers at the rear. A 3-slot card needs 3 of them.
  4. CPU cooler height: 180 mm − 168 mm = 12 mm to spare. Both numbers are measured from the motherboard surface. 12 mm is not much, but it is positive, so the glass panel closes.
  5. Alternative with liquid: a 420 mm radiator (3 × 140 mm) fits in the top or in the front. The datasheet lists both positions. The HWCooling launch report says that the 430 mm card limit also holds with a radiator in the front.

Result: a very large graphics card and a very large air cooler fit, with room to spare.

2. The rule: three intake fans and one exhaust fan replace the air in the case 67 times per minute

The Shadow Base 800 FX is an airflow case. An airflow case has open mesh panels in front of its fans, so the air meets little resistance. Three of the four fans are intake fans (fans that push outside air into the case). They sit behind the front mesh. One fan is an exhaust fan (a fan that pushes the warm air out). It sits at the top of the rear panel.

All four fans are PWM fans. PWM (pulse width modulation) is the speed signal on the fourth wire of a fan connector. It sets the fan speed. The fans connect to a fan hub, a small board with 8 ports behind the motherboard tray. It takes one PWM signal and one ARGB signal from the motherboard and passes them to up to 8 fans or light strips. ARGB (addressable RGB) means that each LED in the fan frame can show its own colour, over a 5 V 3-pin cable.

Worked example: how much air moves
  1. One Light Wings 140 mm PWM fan moves 95.1 m³/h (56 CFM) at 1,500 rpm. This is the be quiet! rating in free air, with nothing in front of the fan.
  2. Intake: 3 × 95.1 = 285.3 m³/h. Exhaust: 95.1 m³/h. The surplus of 190.2 m³/h leaves through the top mesh and the gaps. More air is pushed in than pulled out. This is positive pressure: the pressure inside is a little above the room pressure, so air leaks out, not in. Dust then enters mainly through the filtered intakes.
  3. Volume of the case: 0.550 m × 0.247 m × 0.522 m = 70.9 l. This is the outer box. The usable inside is smaller: be quiet! states 62.74 l in its full table.
  4. 285.3 m³/h = 4,755 l/min. 4,755 ÷ 70.9 = 67 box volumes per minute, one every 0.89 s. The real flow is lower, because the mesh, the dust filter and the parts resist the air. No one measured the real flow.
  5. At 600 rpm (50% of the maximum): 95.1 × 600 ÷ 1,500 = 38.0 m³/h per fan. Intake 114.1 m³/h = 27 box volumes per minute. For one fan, the airflow is proportional to the speed (the first fan affinity law). HWBusters logged 600 rpm at the 50% setting.
  6. Electric power: 4 × 3.8 W = 15.2 W for the motors, 4 × 0.67 A × 5 V = 13.4 W for the LEDs. 28.6 W at most. Values from the be quiet! fan page.

Why the fan speed hardly changes the temperature in this case

With 27 to 67 box volumes per minute, the air inside the case stays close to the room temperature at every fan setting. The parts that then limit the temperature are the CPU cooler and the cooler of the graphics card, not the case. In the HWBusters torture test, with the case fans set to produce 25 dB(A), the CPU reached 88.05 °C. At full fan speed it reached 87.38 °C, which is 0.67 °C less.

KitGuru wrote the same about its test. A higher fan speed made absolutely no difference to the temperature of either the CPU or GPU. The igor'sLAB reviewer ran the case fans at about 700 rpm, which is 44.4 m³/h per fan by the affinity law. A 145 W CPU stayed at 79.8 °C on average, and a 290 W graphics card stayed at 72.3 °C. The consequence for you: set a fan curve near 600 rpm to 700 rpm for normal load.

3. Measured: 7 °C to 10 °C cooler than five other cases, and 12.6 dB(A) at half fan speed

Noise is given as a sound pressure level in dB(A). The level is a logarithmic scale: 10 dB more means 10 times the sound power. The "A" means that the meter weights the frequencies like a human ear. Tom's Hardware measured at 60 cm, HWBusters at 1 m in a chamber without echo. So the two sets of numbers cannot be compared with each other.

Guess first

Compare: how much sound two be quiet! cases hold back

HWBusters also measured how much sound each case holds back. A speaker inside the case played test tones and noise. The noise damping value is this reduction in dB(A). A negative value means the case made the sound louder, because the panels vibrated with the tone. The Shadow Base 800 FX has one insulation mat (a sheet of foam glued to a panel to absorb sound), on the right side panel.

Shadow Base 800 FX (mesh front, mesh top, one mat)

White noise: 5.5 dB(A) quieter.

Tone of 1 kHz: 10.5 dB(A) louder.

Tone of 500 Hz: 14.5 dB(A) louder.

Pure Base 500DX (another be quiet! case)

White noise: 7.1 dB(A) quieter.

Tone of 1 kHz: 9.5 dB(A) quieter.

Tone of 500 Hz: 5.7 dB(A) louder.

The principle: a panel that lets air through also lets sound through. The Shadow Base 800 FX holds back 1.6 dB(A) less white noise than the Pure Base 500DX. At 1 kHz the two differ by 20.0 dB(A). So the low noise of this case in the reviews does not come from its panels. It comes from the fans, which are large, slow and well made.

Then the pressure inside is negative, and dust enters through every gap. If you buy the DX, move the top fan to the front and you get the positive pressure of the FX.

5. Wrong idea corrected: the case is quiet because the fans are slow, not because it blocks sound

Common wrong idea

Wrong: "be quiet! builds silent cases, so the Shadow Base 800 FX is quiet because its panels keep the noise of the parts inside."

Correct: the case has one insulation mat, on the right side panel only. HWBusters measured that the case reduces white noise from inside by only 5.5 dB(A), and that it makes a 1 kHz tone 10.5 dB(A) louder. The quiet results come from the four large fans. At 600 rpm they produce 12.6 dB(A), and the temperature stays within 1 °C.

Why the wrong idea fails: an airflow case must let air pass, and sound passes wherever air passes. A case cannot be both open for air and closed for sound. The Shadow Base 800 series trades sound blocking for airflow, and then wins the noise comparison with slow fans.

7. Words that you will hear

You will meet these words in reviews and in the manual. They are not part of the main idea.

AIO (all-in-one)
A liquid cooler that comes filled and sealed: a pump block on the CPU, two tubes and a radiator with fans.
Static pressure (mm H2O)
The pressure a fan can build against a resistance, for example a radiator. The Light Wings 140 mm PWM has 1.49 mm H2O. A fan for a thick radiator needs more. The case fans of the FX are airflow fans, made for the open mesh.
Dust filter
A fine mesh in front of an intake. The case has filters in the front, in the top and in the bottom. The front filter clips off, the top filter holds with magnets, the bottom filter pulls out to the front.
PSU shroud
The cover over the power supply and the drive cage at the bottom. It hides cables. In this case it carries the air guide of the diagram, and you can remove the cover to fit a thick front radiator.
Vertical GPU mount and riser cable
The slot bracket of this case rotates, so a graphics card can stand with its fans towards the glass. This needs a riser cable (a flexible PCIe extension), which is not included. Tom's Hardware quotes US$20 to US$50 for one.
HDD Cage 2
An extra drive cage that be quiet! sells separately. Two of them fit under the PSU shroud on an included bracket. You need it when you want more than the 3.5-inch trays of the case.
Cable cover and cable bar
A removable cover next to the motherboard that hides the cable openings. You move it to the right for an E-ATX board. Behind it sits a bracket for SSD trays.
Tempered glass
Glass that was heated and cooled fast, so it is stronger and breaks into small pieces. The left panel of this case is 4 mm tinted tempered glass in a steel frame and is held by screws.
Pure Wings 3 and Light Wings
Two fan families of be quiet!. Pure Wings 3 is the plain family and ships in the plain 800 and the DX. Light Wings is the ARGB family and ships in the FX. Silent Wings 4 and Silent Wings Pro 4 are the higher families, sold separately.
4-pin PWM header and 5 V 3-pin ARGB header
The two motherboard connectors that the hub uses. The hub needs one of each. A board without a 5 V ARGB header cannot control the lighting, but the button on the case still can.
Push and pull
Fans on both sides of a radiator, one set that pushes air through and one set that pulls. The 420 mm positions of this case support push and pull, which needs 6 fans for one radiator.
LAeq
The A-weighted sound level averaged over the measurement time. HWBusters reports its noise values as LAeq.
Torture test
A test that loads the CPU and the GPU to 100% at the same time, for example with Prime95 and FurMark. It gives the highest temperatures; games produce less heat.

8. Check your understanding

Try to answer before you open each answer.
  1. The fans run at 1,050 rpm (the 70% setting in the HWBusters test). With the rated airflow and the box volume of 70.9 l, how many box volumes per minute do the three intake fans deliver?
    Show the answer

    About 47 per minute.

    One fan: 95.1 × 1,050 ÷ 1,500 = 66.6 m³/h. Three intake fans: 199.7 m³/h = 3,328 l/min. 3,328 ÷ 70.9 = 46.9. A typical wrong answer uses four fans; the exhaust fan does not add intake. See section 2.

  2. A graphics card is 420 mm long and has its power connector at the far end. Does it fit with the front fans installed?
    Show the answer

    The card fits, with 10 mm to spare. The cable does not fit well.

    430 − 420 = 10 mm. A connector at the end needs room for the cable, for example the 36 mm that NVIDIA asks for the RTX 4090. 10 mm is less than 36 mm, so the cable would bend sharply against the front fans. A typical wrong answer stops at "420 is less than 430".

  3. Which of the five variants include a fan hub, and why does it matter for a build with 7 fans?
    Show the answer

    Only the two FX variants.

    The hub takes one PWM header and one ARGB header from the motherboard and serves up to 8 devices. A build with 7 PWM fans without a hub needs 7 fan headers or splitters. See section 4.

  4. HWBusters measured 88.05 °C on the CPU at the quietest fan setting and 87.38 °C at full fan speed. What does this tell you about the limit of the cooling?
    Show the answer

    The limit is the CPU cooler and the heat of the CPU, not the air in the case.

    More case airflow gave only 0.67 °C. If the case had been the limit, more airflow would have given a larger drop. A typical wrong answer is "the case fans are too weak". See section 2.

9. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  1. The Shadow Base 800 FX is a large airflow mid-tower: 430 mm graphics cards, 180 mm coolers, 420 mm radiators, E-ATX boards, 7 slots. An RTX 4090 Founders Edition fits with 126 mm to spare.
  2. Four Light Wings 140 mm PWM fans, three in and one out, give positive pressure. The rated airflow is 67 box volumes per minute at full speed and 27 at 600 rpm.
  3. In the reviews, the fan speed changed the CPU temperature by less than 1 °C. At 600 rpm the four fans produce 12.6 dB(A) at 1 m. Tom's Hardware measured it 7 °C to 10 °C cooler than five other cases.
  4. The case does not block sound: it reduces white noise from inside by 5.5 dB(A). Its quiet results come from slow, large fans.
  5. The FX cost US$50.00 more than the plain Shadow Base 800 at launch. The extra buys a fourth fan, lit fans and an 8-port hub, not better cooling.

Glossary

Airflow case
A case with open mesh panels in front of its fans, so the air meets little resistance.
ARGB (addressable RGB)
Lighting where each LED can show its own colour, controlled over a 5 V 3-pin cable.
Clearance
The largest part that fits in a given place of a case: the longest graphics card, the highest CPU cooler, the largest radiator.
E-ATX (Extended ATX)
A motherboard format up to 330 mm wide, wider than the 244 mm of ATX.
Exhaust fan
A fan that pushes the warm air out of the case.
Fan hub
A small board that takes one PWM signal and one ARGB signal and passes them to several fans or light strips. The FX hub has 8 ports.
Insulation mat
A sheet of foam glued to a panel to absorb sound. This case has one, on the right side panel.
Intake fan
A fan that pushes outside air into the case.
Mid-tower
A case that stands upright, holds a standard ATX motherboard and is smaller than a full tower. be quiet! writes midi-tower.
Noise damping
The reduction in dB(A) of a sound that comes from inside the case. A negative value means the case made the sound louder.
Positive pressure
The state when the intake fans push more air in than the exhaust fans pull out. Air leaks out through the gaps, so dust enters only through the filtered intakes.
PWM (pulse width modulation)
The speed signal on the fourth wire of a fan connector. It sets the fan speed from a minimum up to 100%.
Radiator
The part of a liquid cooler that gives the heat to the air. Its size is the sum of the fans on it: 420 mm means three 140 mm fans.
Sound pressure level, dB(A)
The loudness of a sound on a logarithmic scale, weighted like the human ear. 10 dB more means 10 times the sound power.
