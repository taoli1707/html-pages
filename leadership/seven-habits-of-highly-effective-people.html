What are the seven habits of highly effective people, and how do you use them at work?

Written 2026-09-30 · Reading time: about 29 minutes · You need to know: nothing special. You have led, or been part of, a software team.

The short answer

The seven habits are a model from the book The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People by Stephen R. Covey, published in 1989. By 2014 the book had sold more than 25 million copies. Covey's claim is that effectiveness comes from character and principles, applied from the inside out, and not from techniques that you apply to other people.

The habits come in three groups. Habits 1 to 3 make you independent. You choose your response, you decide the result that you want, and you spend your time on what is important. Habits 4 to 6 make you work well with other people. You look for results that are good for both sides, you listen before you speak, and you combine different views. Habit 7 renews your capacity, so that the other six habits continue to work.

For an engineer the book has three tools that you will use most. The first is the Circle of Influence from habit 1. The second is the time matrix with its Quadrant II from habit 3. The third is the Emotional Bank Account from habits 4 and 5. The book is a self-help book. Its evidence is Covey's reading and his own experience, and I found no controlled study of its effect on adults.

1. One example: a data-loss bug, 10 days before the release, handled with the seven habits

You lead a team of 5 engineers. The release is planned for 14 October, in 10 days. On Monday at 08:30 a customer reports a bug: in rare cases, the product deletes user data. At 09:00 the product manager writes that the release date cannot move. One engineer told you last week that they want to leave the team. Here is the week, one habit per step.

Two of Covey's terms appear in the first step. The Circle of Concern is the set of all things that you care about. The Circle of Influence is the smaller set of things inside it that you can change. To be proactive, in Covey's meaning, is to take responsibility for your response, and to spend your energy inside the Circle of Influence.

Worked example: the week, one habit per step
  1. Habit 1, be proactive. Before you answer the product manager, you write two lists. You cannot change: the customer report, the calendar, the contract. You can change: the fix, the tests, the plan, and how you speak to the product manager. You work only on the second list. Covey, after the psychiatrist Viktor Frankl, says that between a stimulus and your response lies your ability to choose the response. Energy spent on the first list, the Circle of Concern, changes nothing. Energy spent on the second list, the Circle of Influence, makes that circle larger.
  2. Habit 2, begin with the end in mind. You write the end as one sentence with three parts. "On 14 October we ship a release that cannot delete user data. The product manager trusts the team. Nobody on the team worked more than 40 hours in any week." Covey says that all things are created twice: first in the mind, then in reality. Without a written end, the loudest email of the day decides the goal.
  3. Habit 3, put first things first. You classify the week's work by two questions: is it urgent, and is it important? You cancel 2 status meetings that others requested, and you stop reading the alert channel every 10 minutes. The freed hours go to a test that reproduces the bug, a design review of the fix, and two one-to-one talks. Section 3 shows the hours. Covey's order is: do the urgent and important work first, then spend most of your time on work that is important but not urgent. He calls the cell of his table for this work Quadrant II. The test and the design review are Quadrant II work: not urgent today, but they prevent the next crisis.
  4. Habit 5, seek first to understand, then to be understood. In the meeting with the product manager you ask one question first: "Why can the date not move?" Then you repeat what you heard. A customer contract starts on 14 October, and the customer needs the login feature on that day. Only then do you describe the bug. Covey's order of persuasion is: credibility first, then understanding of the other person, then logic. A person who feels understood is willing to listen. This step comes before habit 4 in time. You cannot propose a result that is good for both sides before you know what the other side needs.
  5. Habit 4, think win-win. You do not say "we need 2 more weeks", and you do not say "we ship with the bug". You propose: ship on 14 October with the code path that deletes data switched off, and ship the full fix on 21 October. The customer gets the login feature on time. The team gets one more week for the fix. Covey says that a result that is good for both sides is a better long-term solution than a result where one side wins. A win for you and a loss for the product manager would cost trust, which you will need at the next release.
  6. Habit 6, synergize. In the team meeting you do not present your plan first. You ask the 5 engineers for options. The engineer who wants to leave knows the code path best, and proposes a switch that disables it in 2 hours of work. Your own plan needed 2 days. Covey says that people who combine their strengths reach results that no one person could reach alone. The difference between the engineers is the source of the better option, not an obstacle.
  7. Habit 7, sharpen the saw. You keep the 2 hours of learning time on Friday afternoon, and you keep your sleep. You do not plan a 60-hour week for anybody. Covey measures a person or a team by the balance between production and the capacity to produce. A 60-hour week raises production for one week and lowers the capacity for the next weeks. Section 2 explains this balance.

Result: the release ships on 14 October without the data-loss path, and the full fix ships on 21 October. The week had 18 hours of important but not urgent work instead of 4. The engineer who wanted to leave was the author of the solution. Whether that engineer stays is not decided by one week, and the book does not promise it.

The steps use the habits in the order 1, 2, 3, 5, 4, 6, 7. The book numbers habit 4 before habit 5, because habit 4 is the attitude and habit 5 is the skill. In a real conversation the listening of habit 5 comes first, and the proposal of habit 4 comes after it.

2. The rule: the habits move you from dependence to interdependence, from the inside out

Covey's model has three ideas under the seven habits. The first idea is the paradigm. A paradigm is the map in your head that you use to see a situation. Covey says that lasting change starts with a change of the paradigm, not with a change of behaviour. This is what he calls change "from the inside out".

In the example, the paradigm changed in step 1. The old paradigm was "the product manager is the problem". The new paradigm was "my response is the only thing that I control".

The second idea is Covey's definition of effectiveness. Effectiveness is the balance between production (P), the results that you get today, and production capability (PC), the capacity that produces results in the future. Covey writes this balance as P/PC, and he says that the whole book rests on it.

His example is a machine. If a factory runs a machine 24 hours a day and skips the maintenance, the output rises for a short time. Then the machine breaks, and the output falls to zero. The machine is the production capability. For a software team, the production capability is the health of the people, the test suite, and the trust between the people.

The third idea is the Maturity Continuum. The Maturity Continuum is Covey's path of three stages. In dependence, other people take care of you. In independence, you take care of yourself. In interdependence, you and other people reach results together. The seven habits are placed on this path.

Covey calls the first group the private victory and the second group the public victory. The order matters to him: you cannot keep an agreement with other people before you can keep an agreement with yourself. Habits 4 and 5 use one more of Covey's terms. The Emotional Bank Account is Covey's picture for the amount of trust in one relationship. Each interaction is a deposit or a withdrawal. Covey lists several kinds of deposit. Five of them are: understanding the other person, attending to small things such as tone of voice, and keeping commitments. The other two are making expectations clear, and apologizing after a withdrawal.

In the example, the question "Why can the date not move?" is a deposit. The sentence "we need 2 more weeks", said first, would be a withdrawal.

Compare the two ways to become effective that Covey contrasts

Covey calls the first way the personality ethic and the second way the character ethic. The book argues for the second way.

Personality ethic: techniques

Learn methods that change how other people see you: how to speak, how to negotiate, how to appear confident.

Works for a short time. Fails when the other person notices the technique.

Character ethic: principles

Change the paradigm and the habits inside yourself: responsibility, a clear end, integrity, trust.

Slow. Covey's claim is that only this way lasts.

The principle after the comparison is the "inside out" rule. A technique applied from the outside, without the paradigm, becomes visible as a technique. Then the Emotional Bank Account loses a deposit instead of gaining one.

3. The data: moving 14 hours into Quadrant II changes the week

Habit 3 uses a table of four cells that Covey calls the time management matrix. The two questions are: is the task urgent, and is the task important? Urgent means that the task demands action now. Important means that the task contributes to the result that you wrote in habit 2. Quadrant II is the cell for tasks that are important but not urgent: planning, prevention, building relationships, learning. Covey says that effective people spend most of their time in this cell.

Guess first

In the example week, before habit 3, the team lead worked 40 hours. How many of those hours were in Quadrant II? Choose a number before you continue: 4 hours, 12 hours, or 20 hours.

Answer to the guess: 4 hours of 40, which is 10%. After habit 3, Quadrant II has 18 hours of 40, which is 45%.

Where did the 14 hours come from? Here is the logic in three steps. First, the 8 hours from Quadrants III and IV came from saying no: two cancelled meetings and a closed alert channel. These hours were free on Monday.

Second, the 6 hours from Quadrant I came later. The reproducing test found the bug on Tuesday instead of Thursday, so the crisis took fewer hours. Third, Covey's claim is that the second effect grows over weeks, because Quadrant II work prevents future crises. In the example, this claim is a plan, not a measurement.

4. Wrong idea corrected: habit 3 is not "do the urgent things first"

Common wrong idea

Wrong: "Put first things first means: sort your tasks by deadline, and do the most urgent task first."

Correct: Covey's first things are the important tasks, and most of them are not urgent. His order is: do the urgent and important tasks when they exist, then spend most of your time in Quadrant II, which is not urgent. He says that many people spend too much time in Quadrants III and IV instead.

Why the wrong idea fails: urgency is often the priority of another person, not yours. A task sorted only by deadline is often a Quadrant III task: a status meeting that someone else requested. A week sorted by urgency leaves no time for the test and the design review. So the next crisis arrives, and the share of Quadrant I grows. In the example, the test that reproduced the bug was not urgent on Monday, and it saved 6 hours of crisis work.

5. Limits: a self-help model with little controlled evidence

Limits
  • The evidence is Covey's reading and experience. Covey studied American self-help books for his doctoral dissertation, and he worked as a teacher and consultant. The book presents principles, not measurements. Covey calls the principles universal. That is his claim, not a tested result.
  • No controlled study with adults. On 30 September 2026 I searched for studies that test the seven habits against a control group. The studies that I found were in schools, and the summaries said that they did not use random assignment. I could not open them, so this point has the label Low.
  • The habits are a framework, not a procedure. The book does not say how to switch off a code path or how to run a design review. It says which work deserves the hours.
  • The example is one week. Covey's claim that Quadrant II time reduces crises is a claim about months. One week cannot show it.
  • The names are trademarks. FranklinCovey, the company that sells trainings based on Covey's books, offers a course with the same name and marks the habit names as registered trademarks. A training at your company is a product of that company, and its content can differ from the book.

6. Words that you will hear

These terms appear in trainings and in conversations about the book. They are not needed for the main idea, but you will meet them next to it.

Eisenhower matrix
Another name for the four-cell table of urgent and important. The name refers to the American president Dwight Eisenhower. Relation to the main idea: Covey's time management matrix in habit 3 is the same table with Covey's advice about Quadrant II.
Personal mission statement
A written text of a few sentences that states who you want to be and what you want to contribute. Relation to the main idea: it is the tool of habit 2 for a whole life, where the example used one sentence for one release.
Paradigm shift
A change of the map in your head, after which the same facts look different. Relation to the main idea: Covey's "inside out" change starts with a paradigm shift.
Abundance mentality
The belief that there is enough success for everyone, so another person's win does not reduce yours. Relation to the main idea: Covey names it as the condition for habit 4. Its opposite is the scarcity mentality.
Win-win or no deal
Covey's rule for the case where no result is good for both sides: agree to make no agreement, instead of accepting a loss. Relation to the main idea: it is the limit case of habit 4.
Empathic listening
Listening with the intent to understand the other person's view and feelings, not with the intent to reply. Relation to the main idea: it is the skill of habit 5.
The Leader in Me
A programme by FranklinCovey, from Covey's 2008 book of the same name, that teaches the seven habits in schools. Relation to the main idea: the school studies that I found in the limits section are about this programme.

7. Check your understanding

Try to answer before you open each answer.
  1. A colleague says: "I am proactive. When an alert fires, I jump on it within one minute." Is this habit 1 in Covey's meaning?
    Show the answer

    Not by itself.

    Covey's "proactive" means choosing your response and working inside the Circle of Influence. Fast reaction to every alert is a reaction to a stimulus, and most alerts are Quadrant III: urgent, but not important. The typical wrong answer is "yes, because the colleague takes initiative". Initiative is part of the word in everyday English, but Covey's test is the choice of response, not the speed. See section 1, step 1.

  2. Your week has 40 hours. You spend 20 hours on crises, 12 hours on meetings that others requested, 6 hours on a test suite, and 2 hours reading news. Which quadrant does Covey want you to grow, and what is its share now?
    Show the answer

    Quadrant II, which is now 6 hours of 40, or 15%.

    The test suite is important and not urgent, so it is Quadrant II. The crises are Quadrant I (20 hours, 50%). The requested meetings are Quadrant III (12 hours, 30%). The news is Quadrant IV (2 hours, 5%).

    Covey says: do the Quadrant I work, then move hours from Quadrants III and IV into Quadrant II. The typical wrong answer is Quadrant I, because it feels most important. See section 3.

  3. Why does Covey number habit 4 (win-win) before habit 5 (understand first), when the worked example uses habit 5 first?
    Show the answer

    Habit 4 is the attitude, and habit 5 is the skill that applies it in a conversation. The book orders the habits by what you must have, not by the order of a meeting. In a real meeting you listen first. You cannot propose a result that is good for both sides before you know what the other side needs. See the paragraph after the worked example in section 1.

  4. A team ships on time for 6 months by working 55 hours per week. Covey would say that this team is not effective. Which of his definitions is the reason?
    Show the answer

    The P/PC balance.

    Effectiveness is the balance between production (shipping on time) and production capability (the health of the people, the tests, the trust). 55-hour weeks raise production and reduce production capability, like a machine that runs without maintenance. The typical wrong answer names habit 3, because hours are involved. Habit 3 is about which work gets the hours; the P/PC balance is about the capacity itself. See section 2.

8. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  1. Covey's effectiveness is the balance between results today and the capacity to produce results tomorrow.
  2. Habits 1 to 3 make you independent: choose your response, decide the end, spend the hours on important work. Habits 4 to 6 make you work with others: results for both sides, listen first, combine strengths. Habit 7 renews the capacity.
  3. Change comes from the inside out: a paradigm first, then behaviour. A technique without the paradigm costs trust.
  4. The most used tool is the time matrix. In the example, Quadrant II grew from 4 hours to 18 hours of 40. The hours came from cancelled meetings and from a test that shortened the crisis.
  5. The book is a self-help book with more than 25 million copies sold by 2014. I found no controlled study of its effect on adults.

Glossary

Circle of Concern
The set of all things that you care about, including the things that you cannot change.
Circle of Influence
The subset of the Circle of Concern that you can change.
Effectiveness
Covey's definition: the balance between production (results today) and production capability (the capacity that produces results in the future). Written P/PC.
Emotional Bank Account
Covey's picture for the amount of trust in one relationship. Each interaction is a deposit or a withdrawal.
Maturity Continuum
Covey's path of three stages: dependence, independence, interdependence.
Paradigm
The map in your head that you use to see a situation.
Proactive
Covey's meaning: taking responsibility for your response, and spending your energy inside the Circle of Influence.
Quadrant II
The cell of the time management matrix for tasks that are important but not urgent.
