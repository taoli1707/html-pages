How do you form Livyden Group LLC in New Jersey to publish the app?

Written 2026-09-29, name changed to Livyden Group on 2026-10-01 · Reading time: about 24 minutes · You need to know: nothing about company law; you need a New Jersey street address and your Social Security number

The short answer

Livyden Group LLC exists after three filings. You file them online, in this order, in about one hour of your own time.

  1. Certificate of Formation with the New Jersey Division of Revenue: US$100. This creates the company.
  2. Employer Identification Number from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS): $0. This is the tax number of the company.
  3. Business registration (form NJ-REG) with New Jersey: $0, due within 60 days of the first filing. This registers the company for state tax.

After that, the company costs $75 per year for the state annual report. The name Livyden Group was available in the New Jersey business name search on 2026-10-01, and no United States trademark contains the word Livyden. The word "Group" is part of the name for one reason. The state search refuses the shorter name "Livyden", because a dental practice named LIVY DENTAL LLC is on record, see section 4.

Assumptions on this page
  • You are the only owner. The company is a single-member LLC.
  • Your home address in New Jersey is the address of the company and of its registered agent.
  • The company has no employees and collects no New Jersey sales tax. Apple collects the sales tax on App Store sales, see "Words that you will hear".
  • The company keeps the default tax treatment. You do not elect corporation or S-corporation tax treatment.
  • The example dates assume that you file the Certificate of Formation on 5 October 2026.

1. One example: the three filings for Livyden Group LLC, step by step

A limited liability company (LLC) is a company that the state creates when you file one document. The company is a legal person that is separate from you. It can own the app, sign the Apple contract, and receive the App Store money. If the company owes money, the creditor can take the money of the company, but not your house or your savings. This protection is the "limited liability" in the name.

The owner of an LLC is called a member. Livyden Group LLC has one member: you. Below is the complete sequence, with the values that you enter.

Worked example: Livyden Group LLC, filed on 5 October 2026
  1. Check the name. Open the New Jersey business name search and enter "Livyden Group", without "LLC". On 2026-10-01 the search answered Livyden Group is available!. New Jersey requires that a name is distinguishable from every name already on record in the state. Distinguishable means: not the same and not nearly the same. The search reads that record.
  2. File the Certificate of Formation. Open the New Jersey Business Formation portal. Choose "Domestic Limited Liability Company". Enter the name Livyden Group LLC and your home address as the main address. Enter yourself as the registered agent, the person who receives legal documents for the company, at the same address. Enter a business purpose ("development and publication of software applications") and the industry code 513210 (Software Publishers). Pay $100 by card. The Certificate of Formation is the one document that creates the company. The fee is $100 for every for-profit entity, as the state page "Getting Registered" states. The state gives the company a 10-digit Entity ID. Keep the approved certificate as a PDF; the bank and Apple ask for it.
  3. Get the Employer Identification Number. Open the free IRS EIN Assistant. Choose "Limited Liability Company", 1 member, state New Jersey, reason "Started a new business". Enter yourself as the responsible party with your Social Security number. Enter 0 employees. Choose "Receive letter online". Cost: $0. The number arrives at once. The Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax number of the company. Your Social Security number is your own tax number in the same way. The IRS states that most new single-member LLCs need one, and banks require one to open a company account. Only the IRS site is free; the paid sites in search results file the same free form.
  4. Register for state tax with form NJ-REG. Open the New Jersey business registration portal. Enter the Entity ID, the EIN, the name, and the start date. Answer "No" to employees, "No" to sales tax collection, and "No" to payroll withholding. Cost: $0. Deadline: 4 December 2026, which is 60 days after 5 October 2026. The state form says that form NJ-REG must be submitted within 60 days of filing the new business entity. The state then issues the Business Registration Certificate (BRC), a document that proves the registration. Leave every "register for this tax" box empty, because each box creates a filing duty that the company does not have.
  5. Every following year: file the annual report. The first one is due on 31 October 2027, and then on the last day of October every year. Cost: $75. The annual report is a yearly confirmation of the address and the registered agent, with a fee. The state page says the report is due on the last day of the month in which you formed the company. A company that skips the report for two consecutive years can lose its charter.

Result: three filings, $100 in total, about one hour of work. The state keeps three facts about the company: its name, its address with a registered agent, and its tax registration.

Two things belong to the same week but are not state filings. First, open a company bank account with the certificate and the EIN, and pay every company cost from it. Second, sign a one-page operating agreement, see "Words that you will hear". Neither is filed with the state.

2. The rule: an LLC is a separate legal person, and the state must know three things about it

The example above follows one rule. New Jersey creates an LLC when it knows three things. The first is a distinguishable name. The second is an address with an agent who accepts legal documents. The third is a tax registration. Each filing in section 1 supplies one of these facts.

The registered agent receives legal documents for the company, for example a court summons. The New Jersey statute states who may serve. The agent is an individual who is a resident of this State, or a business with authority to transact business in this State. You are a resident, so you can be the agent yourself.

The address of the agent is the registered office. It must be a street address in New Jersey, and it becomes public record. Companies that do not want a home address in public record pay a commercial agent, but this page assumes your home address.

Single-member LLC compared with the alternative

The alternative to an LLC is no company at all. Then you publish the app as an individual, which the law calls a sole proprietorship. The two cases differ in one feature: whether a separate legal person exists.

No company (sole proprietorship)

You sign the Apple contract yourself. Your own name is the seller name on the App Store.

A creditor of the business can take your personal savings.

State cost: $0. Apple enrolment: individual.

Livyden Group LLC (single-member LLC)

The company signs the Apple contract. "Livyden Group LLC" is the seller name.

A creditor of the company can take only the money of the company, if you keep the money separate.

State cost: $100, then $75 per year. Apple enrolment: organization.

The principle: the state fee buys the separation between your money and the money of the business. The separation holds only if you keep it. If you pay personal costs from the company account, a court can ignore the separation. Lawyers call this "piercing the veil".

4. The name: Livyden Group passes both checks, but plain Livyden fails the state check

Two different systems check a company name, and they answer two different questions.

The first system is the state record. New Jersey requires that the name is distinguishable from every name on record in the state, see section 1. The statute also requires that the name contains "limited liability company" or the abbreviation "L.L.C." or "LLC". So the legal name is "Livyden Group LLC". The state search on 2026-10-01 reported that Livyden Group is available. This check is complete.

The same search refuses the shorter name "Livyden". On 2026-10-01 it answered Not Available and listed one similar business on record, LIVY DENTAL LLC, a dental practice. The search compares names with the spaces and the suffix removed. So it compares LIVYDEN with LIVYDENTAL and sees one as the beginning of the other. The online formation portal runs this same search before it accepts a filing, so "Livyden LLC" cannot be filed online. The word "Group" changes the comparison: LIVYDENGROUP is not the beginning of LIVYDENTAL, and the search accepts it.

The second system is the federal trademark register. A trademark is a name or sign that identifies the source of goods or services. A registered trademark gives its owner the right to stop others from using a confusingly similar name for related goods. The state does not check trademarks when it accepts a company name. So a name can be available in New Jersey and still conflict with a trademark.

The trademark register of the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) contains no mark that includes the word LIVYDEN. On 2026-10-01 a search for marks that contain the word returned 0 live marks and 0 dead marks. A web search for the word on the same day found no company, product, or person with this name. The nearest existing words are different: Livyatan, an extinct whale, and LIVDEN, a tile brand in California with its own registered trademark. Those words would matter only if you chose them, and this page does not.

What this means for you. A trademark conflict exists only when a buyer could confuse two sources. No registered mark contains the word Livyden, so nobody holds a federal right to stop you from using it. Three practical consequences follow:

  • The New Jersey filing can go ahead with "Livyden Group LLC". Enter the name with the word "Group" in every filing, because the state record stores the full name.
  • If you later want to register "Livyden" as your own trademark for software, the examiner will find no earlier mark with this word. The examiner can still refuse a mark for other reasons, for example a similar sound to another mark. So a clean search is not a guarantee.
  • A trademark search finds registered marks only. A company that uses a name without registration can still have rights in its own area. The web search on 2026-10-01 found no such company, but a web search is not complete.

5. Wrong idea corrected: a single-member LLC does not change your income tax

Common wrong idea

Wrong: "The company pays its own tax at a company rate, so an LLC reduces my tax."

Correct: the IRS treats a single-member LLC as a disregarded entity. This means that, for income tax, the IRS ignores the company and taxes you directly. The IRS page states that an LLC with one member is treated as an entity disregarded as separate from its owner. The profit of Livyden Group LLC goes on Schedule C of your own Form 1040, added to your salary. The profit is also subject to self-employment tax.

Why the wrong idea fails: the liability protection of an LLC comes from state law. The tax treatment comes from federal law, and federal law gives a single-member LLC no tax of its own by default. The LLC changes who signs contracts and who owes debts. It does not change how much income tax you pay.

The same rule has a practical effect on the Apple tax form. The IRS states that a disregarded LLC must give the owner's Social Security number, not the EIN of the LLC, on a Form W-9. So the W-9 in App Store Connect shows your own name on line 1 and "Livyden Group LLC" on line 2. The taxpayer number on the form is your Social Security number.

6. Limits: the employer clause, fees that change, and what this page is not

Limits
  • The employer clause comes first. Many employment contracts contain an invention-assignment clause. Such a clause can give the employer the rights to software that you write, even at home. An LLC cannot own code that your employer already owns. Get written confirmation from your employer before you pay the $100.
  • Fees and forms change. The formation fee was $125 before 1 July 2026 and is $100 now. Trust the fee shown on the state page on the day you file.
  • Processing time is not verified. The state pages that this page cites do not state how many days the online formation takes. No number is available.
  • The state search is an automated check, not the final decision. The result for "Livyden Group" came from the public search tool, which the online portal also uses. A staff member of the Division of Revenue can still reject a name when the filing is reviewed. No number is available for how often this happens.
  • This page is not legal or tax advice. It explains the public filings and cites the state and federal pages. The employer clause and the trademark question are the two points where one hour with a lawyer is worth the fee.
  • Insurance is not covered. An LLC limits liability, but it does not replace insurance. This page does not evaluate insurance.

7. Words that you will hear

These terms belong to the weeks after the formation. They are not part of the three filings, but you will meet them.

Operating agreement
A written contract between the members of an LLC about ownership and management. With one member it is a one-page statement: you own 100%, you manage the company, the tax treatment is disregarded entity. Nobody files it, but banks ask for it, and it supports the separation between you and the company.
NAICS code
A six-digit industry code of the North American Industry Classification System. The state and the IRS ask for it. For an app publisher the code is 513210, Software Publishers.
D-U-N-S number
A free nine-digit company identifier issued by Dun and Bradstreet. Apple requires it to enrol an organization. Apple says to allow up to 5 business days to receive it, plus 2 business days for Apple to see it. Apply after the Certificate of Formation is approved, because the number is registered to the legal entity.
Apple Developer Program, organization enrolment
The membership that lets the company publish apps. It costs 99 US dollars per membership year. Apple requires four things. These are a legal entity, a D-U-N-S number, a working website on a domain of the company, and a person who can bind the company. As the owner you have that authority. The LLC is the legal entity that makes this enrolment possible.
Form W-9
The IRS form that tells Apple your taxpayer number. For a disregarded LLC, the IRS requires the owner's Social Security number, see section 5.
Schedule C and self-employment tax
Schedule C is the part of your personal tax return that reports the profit of the business. Self-employment tax is the Social Security and Medicare tax on that profit, which an employer would otherwise withhold. Both follow from the disregarded-entity rule in section 5.
Marketplace facilitator
A platform that sells on behalf of many sellers and collects the sales tax itself. Apple acts as one for the App Store. This is why the NJ-REG form in section 1 answers "No" to sales tax collection. Confidence: Medium, see the sources.
Beneficial ownership information (BOI) report
A federal report about the owners of a company that FinCEN required from 2024. FinCEN's page now states that US companies are exempt. Livyden Group LLC files nothing.
Pass-through
Another word for the same rule as disregarded entity: the profit passes through the company to your own tax return.

8. Check your understanding

Try to answer before you open each answer.
  1. You file the Certificate of Formation on 20 November 2026. What is the last day for form NJ-REG, and when is the first annual report due?
    Show the answer

    NJ-REG by 19 January 2027. First annual report by 30 November 2027.

    NJ-REG is due within 60 days of the formation. 20 November plus 60 days is 19 January. The annual report is due on the last day of the formation month in the following year, so 30 November 2027. A typical wrong answer is 31 December 2027, because people expect a calendar-year deadline.

  2. The New Jersey search says that a name is available. Does this mean that nobody can stop you from using the name?
    Show the answer

    No. The state search checks only the state record of company names. A federal trademark is a separate right, and the state does not check it. For Livyden Group, both checks pass. The opposite case also exists: plain "Livyden" has no trademark conflict, but the state record refuses it because of LIVY DENTAL LLC. See section 4.

  3. Livyden Group LLC earns US$5,000 of profit in 2027. Who pays the income tax on it, and on which form?
    Show the answer

    You do, on Schedule C of your own Form 1040. The IRS treats a single-member LLC as a disregarded entity. The company files no income tax return of its own. The typical wrong answer is "the company pays at a corporate rate". That is true only after a tax election, and this page assumes you do not make one.

Glossary

Annual report
A yearly confirmation of the address and the registered agent of the company, filed with New Jersey with a fee of US$75.
Business Registration Certificate (BRC)
The document that New Jersey issues after form NJ-REG. It proves that the company is registered for state tax.
Certificate of Formation
The one document that creates a New Jersey LLC. Fee: US$100.
Disregarded entity
The default federal tax treatment of a single-member LLC: the IRS ignores the company and taxes the owner directly.
Distinguishable
The state requirement for a company name: not the same and not nearly the same as a name already on record.
Employer Identification Number (EIN)
The federal tax number of a company, issued free by the IRS.
Limited liability company (LLC)
A company created by state filing, which is a legal person separate from its owner. The creditors of the company cannot take the personal property of the owner.
Member
An owner of an LLC.
Registered agent
The person who receives legal documents for the company. In New Jersey, a resident individual or a business authorized in the state.
Registered office
The New Jersey street address of the registered agent. It is public record.
Trademark
A name or sign that identifies the source of goods or services. A registered trademark lets its owner stop confusingly similar use for related goods.
