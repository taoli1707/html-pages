How does Mirko Romanelli design a product, and how was his website designed?

Written 2026-10-11 · Reading time: about 30 minutes · You need to know: what a 3D model and a web page are

The short answer

Mirko Romanelli is a product and industrial designer in Florence, Italy. He designs a product in four steps: analysis and research, concept, 3D model, and rendering with a presentation. One idea gives the product its form. The material and the small details give it its character. His twelve published projects use steel, marble, travertine, oak, and recycled plastic.

He did not design his own website. The agency Shakuro designed and built it in 2024. Shakuro started from research and a logo, chose a palette with no accent colour, built the site in Webflow, and added custom animation code. The site won a Site of the Day award on 18 December 2024. On 18 February 2025 it won a Special Kudos award with a judges' score of 7.48 of 10.

Assumptions on this page

Part A

1. One product: the Oscar wardrobe, painted steel, 14.5 kg, EUR 349

Short answer for Part A

Romanelli works in four steps: research, concept, 3D model, rendering. The concept is one simple form that you can recognise at once. The material and the details then carry the quality. Many of his projects are concept studies with no manufacturer.

Oscar is an open wardrobe that Romanelli designed with Anna Fiore for the Italian furniture brand Hiro Design. It is the project with the most published numbers, so it is the main example of this page.

The product is a piece of industrial design. Industrial design is the design of an object that a factory makes in many copies: its form, material, parts, and joints.

Worked example: Oscar from the public facts
  1. The need. A wardrobe for an entrance or a bedroom, with a shelf and a bar for hangers. It must stand on its own and must not damage the floor. The Hiro page lists these uses and the hard rubber feet.
  2. The concept. Two side frames, two tubular bars, and one shelf. Nothing else. The product arrives disassembled in these five parts, and screws join them. A small number of parts is the visible form of what Romanelli calls the "essentiality of forms". Five parts also pack flat.
  3. The material. Painted steel with a polyester powder coating. A powder coating is a dry paint powder that is sprayed on the metal and then baked hard in an oven. Hiro states that the surface is scratch-resistant and holds its colour. Steel tube gives a thin frame that carries a load. The coating gives colour and protection in one layer.
  4. The dimensions. Width 100 cm, depth 58 cm, height 160.5 cm. Weight 14.5 kg. Maximum load 20 kg, evenly distributed. The bar leaves 71 cm of space for hangers. The shelf is 44.7 cm by 95.5 cm. These values come from the 3D model, where the designer fixes every size before the factory sees the design.
  5. The colours. Seven colours. Six are standard RAL colours (a European catalogue of numbered paint colours, for example RAL 9001 "Shell White"). One is a custom code for "Midnight Blue". A RAL number lets any paint shop mix the same colour. The frame is one shape; only the coating changes.
  6. The production. Price EUR 349. Hiro makes the product after the order and delivers in 4 to 6 weeks. This is on-demand production: the factory makes one unit for each order. There is no stock, so the brand can offer seven colours at no extra cost.

Result: a product with five parts, one material, one surface finish, and seven colours. The idea is simple. The specification is exact.

2. The rule for products: one concept, then material and detail

Romanelli states his process in one sentence on his site. The sentence is: Analysis and research, development of concepts, ideas and projects, creation of 3D models, product rendering and presentation. That is four steps. The diagram shows them with the Oscar values.

The four steps are common to every industrial designer. Specific to Romanelli is the rule inside step 2. He writes that he creates products expressed through the simplicity of the form and the iconization of the concept. He calls this the iconization of the concept: the product has one idea, and its form is a picture of that idea.

Three projects show the rule with their names. Capitello is a metal storage table shaped like the capital, the top block of a classical column. Nest is a pair of marble salt and pepper shakers that sit in one base, like eggs in a nest. Kombo is a desk organiser in recycled plastic whose stepped form comes from the rice terraces of Yuanyang and Yunhe in China. In each case one sentence states the idea, and the form shows it.

After the concept, the material does the rest. His own words on the home page are: Minimal design, enhanced by details and materials. The Oscar example shows what "details" means in practice. The details are rubber feet, a shelf size, a 20 kg load limit, and a coating that holds its colour. None of these change the form. All of them decide whether the product works for ten years.

Compare a concept study with a product for sale

Ten of the twelve projects on his site name no client. These are concept studies: complete designs with a 3D model and renderings, but no factory and no price. The two cases differ in one feature: a brand that orders the product.

PS 2.0, a concept study

A redesign of the PlayStation 2 Slim console, published 11 December 2021.

Steps 1 to 4 only. The goal is to keep the identity of the console and update some features. No manufacturer, no price, no dimensions published.

Oscar, a product for sale

An open wardrobe for Hiro Design, EUR 349, seven colours.

Steps 1 to 5. The brand adds a price, a delivery time, a load limit, and an assembly guide. The published facts are exact because a customer can check them.

The principle: the method is the same in both cases. The difference is who checks the result: the designer for a study, the customer for a product. So the exact numbers come from the products for sale.

4. Wrong idea corrected: minimal design is not less work

Common wrong idea

Wrong: "A minimal product has few parts, so the design takes less work than a complex product."

Correct: the form takes less time to draw. The details take more. Oscar has five parts. Its specification still has a load limit, a shelf size, a hanger length, a hook length, rubber feet, seven colour codes, and a coating type.

Why the wrong idea fails: on a complex product, a weak detail hides among many others. On a product with five parts, every joint, every edge, and every surface is visible. Romanelli says this himself: Capitello is simple and compact at first glance but studied down to the smallest detail at a closer look. The work moves from the form to the details; it does not disappear.

  1. A customer orders an Oscar wardrobe in Terracotta. Which step of the four produced the number "RAL 3012", and why does a standard number matter?
    Show the answer

    Step 4, rendering and presentation, chose the colour. The RAL number matters because any paint supplier can mix it exactly.

    The frame is the same in all seven colours, so the colour is a presentation choice, not a form choice. A typical wrong answer is step 3, the 3D model. The model fixes the sizes, not the paint.

  2. Litus and Kombo both come from a material or a landscape. Which one applies the "iconization of the concept" rule, and which one starts from the material?
    Show the answer

    Kombo applies the rule: the stepped form is a picture of rice terraces. Litus starts from the material: the waste blocks decide the form.

    Both are minimal. The order of the steps differs. In Kombo the concept comes first and the recycled plastic serves it. In Litus the available blocks come first and the bench form serves them.

Part B

5. The website: Shakuro built it in seven steps

Short answer for Part B

Romanelli did not build his site. The agency Shakuro did, and his site says "Made by Shakuro" in its footer. Shakuro worked in seven steps: research, logo, brand identity system, wireframes, user interface design, a Webflow build, and custom animation code. One decision shapes the whole site: a palette with no accent colour. The product images are then the only colour on the page.

The site is at mirkoromanelli.com, with four pages: Home, About, Works, and Contact. The source of this section is Shakuro's own case study. I checked it against the HTML of the live site. HTML is the text file that a browser turns into a page.

Worked example: the seven steps of the site, with Shakuro's reasons
  1. Research. Shakuro collected the client's preferences, style guidelines, brand attributes, and user personas. From these it made stylescapes. A stylescape is one wide image that collects colours, type, photos, and layout fragments to show one possible visual direction. It is cheaper to reject an image than a finished page design.
  2. Logo. Shakuro tried different shapes, line weights, and typefaces, and gave Romanelli a set of logo options to choose from. The live site uses the mark "MKRM". The logo is the smallest element. The rest of the identity follows its weight and proportions.
  3. Brand identity system. A brand identity system is the set of rules for a brand's type, colours, spacing, and other visual elements. Shakuro first explored colour variations. Then it chose a palette with no accent colour at all. Shakuro gives the reason. This decision allows Mirko's work to take center stage on the website. A marble or terracotta product is the only colour on a grey page.
  4. Wireframes. A wireframe is a page layout drawn with boxes and lines, without colour or final text. Shakuro drew the wireframes at the same time as the identity system. Structure and style were designed together, so neither had to be redone.
  5. User interface design. User interface (UI) design, from here UI design, is how a page looks and how its controls are arranged. The home page uses a rhythmic, flowing arrangement of projects with no strict hierarchy. The Works page moves beyond a traditional grid. Different image sizes invite the visitor to scroll. Equal tiles look like a catalogue.
  6. Webflow build. Webflow is a visual tool that builds a website without hand-written code and hosts it. Shakuro used its content management system (from here: CMS), which stores each project as a record and generates one page per record. Romanelli can add a thirteenth project as a record, without the agency.
  7. Custom code. Webflow's built-in animations were not enough for the planned effects. Shakuro added GSAP (GreenSock Animation Platform, a JavaScript library that moves elements on a timeline) for transitions. For the header it wrote a WebGL fragment shader. A fragment shader is a small program that runs on the graphics processor and computes the colour of every pixel. This shader produces a 3D blur effect. The graphics processor computes all pixels in parallel, so the blur stays smooth.

Result: a grey site with one typeface and no accent colour. The product images are the only colour. A separate script adds the motion.

6. The rule for the website: the work is the colour

Shakuro's seven steps follow the product rule of Part A. For a product, the form is simple and the material gives the character. For the site, the layout and the palette are neutral, and the product images give the character. Shakuro states this in its case study. The palette has no accent colour. The reason given is this: A neutral color palette was chosen to keep the design flexible and adaptable to various types of products.

The contrast with a standard portfolio is one feature: a standard grid has equal tiles and an accent colour on links. Here the images differ in size, and the only colour is the product. Remove every colour that is not the product, and the product becomes the accent.

Check Part B before you continue.
  1. Romanelli wants to add a thirteenth project next month. Which of the seven steps must be repeated, and which not?
    Show the answer

    None of the design steps. He adds one CMS record in Webflow (step 6), and the existing templates and scripts apply to it.

    Steps 1 to 5 defined the rules once. Step 7's code reads the page structure, so a new project heading with the same attribute gets the same animation. A typical wrong answer is "step 5, because the new page must be designed". The page template already exists.

  2. A client asks you for "a minimal site like this one, so it should be light". What do the numbers of section 7 say?
    Show the answer

    The look is minimal; the code is not. The custom script alone is 355 KB, and it loads GSAP, two plug-ins, Lenis, and a shader.

    If "light" means a small download, you must drop the motion and keep only Webflow, one typeface, and the palette. If "light" means the look, the motion can stay. The two meanings need different budgets.

9. Limits: what the sources do not say

Limits
  • No sketches, hours, or tools for the products. The designer publishes no sketch, no software name, and no project duration. Step 2 of the worked example is read from the finished product.
  • Oscar is a two-person design. Hiro credits it to Mirko Romanelli and Anna Fiore. The public pages do not say which decisions were whose.
  • The website case study is written by the agency. Its technical claims held against the live HTML. Its process claims, for example that two steps ran in parallel, cannot be checked.
  • The palette is read from a compiled stylesheet. It holds a dark grey, two off-whites, two mid greys, and two blues that are Webflow defaults. I did not confirm which appear on screen.
  • Two award pages gave only a title. The FWA page and the Behance profile returned no readable body text to my tools. So the FWA award and the Behance statistics are not on this page as facts.
  • The site changes. The script size and the library versions are as read on 11 October 2026.

10. Check your understanding

Two questions that join the two parts.
  1. State in one sentence the rule that Part A and Part B share.
    Show the answer

    Keep the form or the layout neutral, and let one element carry the character: the material for a product, the product image for the site.

    Romanelli's words for the product are "minimal design, enhanced by details and materials". Shakuro's words for the site are a palette with "no accent colors" so the work takes "center stage". A typical wrong answer names only "minimal", which describes the form but not where the character comes from.

  2. Who designed the website, and how do you know from the site itself?
    Show the answer

    The agency Shakuro. The footer of every page says "Made by Shakuro", and both award listings credit Shakuro.

    A typical wrong answer is "Romanelli, because it is his portfolio". He supplied the brand and the content; the agency designed and built the pages.

11. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  1. Romanelli designs a product in four steps: research, concept, 3D model, rendering. A fifth step, production, exists only when a brand orders the product.
  2. The concept is one idea that the form shows at once. The material and the details then carry the quality: Oscar has five parts, one coating, seven RAL colours, and a 20 kg load limit.
  3. The agency Shakuro designed and built the website in seven steps. The deciding choice was no accent colour, so the product images are the only colour.
  4. The calm page runs on a 355 KB custom script with GSAP 3.12.5, Lenis, and a WebGL shader. The look is minimal. The code is not.
  5. The site scored 7.48 from the CSS Design Awards judges and 7.9 on Orpetron; both credit Shakuro.

Glossary

Brand identity system
The set of rules for a brand's type, colours, spacing, and other visual elements.
Concept study
A complete design with a 3D model and renderings, but with no factory and no price.
Content management system (CMS)
A part of a website tool that stores each item, for example a project, as a record and generates a page from it.
GSAP
GreenSock Animation Platform, a JavaScript library that moves page elements along a timeline.
Iconization of the concept
Romanelli's term: the form of the product is a picture of its one idea, so the product becomes a symbol of that idea.
Industrial design
The design of an object that a factory makes in many copies: its form, material, parts, and joints.
On-demand production
The factory makes one unit for each order; there is no stock.
Powder coating
A dry paint powder that is sprayed on metal and baked hard in an oven.
RAL colour
A colour from a European catalogue of numbered paint colours, for example RAL 9001.
Stylescape
One wide image that collects colours, type, photos, and layout fragments to show a possible visual direction.
UI design
User interface design: how a page looks and how its controls are arranged.
UX design
User experience design: how the whole visit feels, from the first page to the last.
Webflow
A visual tool that builds and hosts a website without hand-written code.
WebGL fragment shader
A small program that runs on the graphics processor and computes the colour of every pixel.
Wireframe
A page layout drawn with boxes and lines, without colour or final text.
